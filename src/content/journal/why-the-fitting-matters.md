People assume the skill in tailoring is the measuring. It is the part that looks technical: the tape, the numbers, the notebook. In truth the measuring is the easy half. Numbers describe a body at rest, standing still, in a warm room, being polite about it.

The hard part is what happens after, when the garment exists and goes on you for the first time.

## A body does not stand still

Almost nobody is symmetrical. One shoulder sits lower than the other, usually the writing hand. Most men stand with a little more weight on one leg. Necks come forward, backs round, arms hang at their own angle rather than the one a pattern assumes.

None of that shows in a set of measurements. All of it shows the moment the jacket is on and you reach for a door handle, or sit down, or forget you are being looked at.

That is what a fitting is for: not confirming the numbers, but catching everything the numbers could not say.

## What actually happens at the fitting

The garment comes to the fitting finished rather than half-built, and it is corrected on your body. Sam looks at where the collar sits against the neck, whether the shoulder is clean or has a divot, how the sleeve hangs relative to the natural forward pitch of your arm, where the trouser breaks on the shoe you actually brought.

Then it is marked and put right. That is the step people are paying for, though almost nobody realises it when they walk in.

## When a build is harder to fit

Some bodies do not forgive a single pass. A very sloped shoulder, a big drop between chest and waist, a stoop, a build that has never once worked off the rack. For those, Sam will make a sample suit first and try that on you, so the problems get found on cloth that does not matter before anything is cut from cloth that does.

It is a slower route and he will tell you if yours needs it. Most do not.

## Why this is the argument against buying online

An online made-to-measure service takes your numbers and posts you a garment. There is no step where anyone looks at you in it. If it is wrong, and for a non-average body it very often is, the correction loop is a courier and a fortnight, repeated until you give up. More on that in [off the rack versus custom](/guides/off-the-rack-vs-custom) and [how a suit should fit](/guides/how-a-suit-should-fit).

The pattern is kept afterwards, corrections and all, which is why the second suit is easier than the first and the fifth needs no fitting at all.
