Plenty of tailors advertise a house call. Here's the straight answer about this one: Sam works from the studio in Vaughan, and that's where he'd rather measure you. He has gone out to a client before and he may do it again, but it isn't a service on the menu, and he decides each time whether he can do it justice. Ask, and you'll get a yes or a no rather than a maybe.

## Why the fitting happens at the studio

Because a fitting is not just measuring. It's a wall of cloth in daylight, a three-way mirror, a pinning surface, and a chalk that has to go on straight. A living room gives you none of that, and every one of them changes the garment.

The light alone decides things. A navy that looks black under a kitchen bulb reads properly by the studio window, and men change their mind about cloth constantly once they see it honestly. Choosing a suit under the wrong light is how people end up with a colour they never quite loved.

## What you get for the drive

- **The full range in one room.** Not a sample book carried in a bag: the actual bolts, so you can put two greys side by side and see which one is yours.
- **Mirrors that tell the truth.** You cannot judge a shoulder line on a phone camera or a hallway mirror.
- **One client at a time.** The room is booked for you, so nothing is rushed and nobody is waiting behind you.
- **The whole party in one block.** A wedding group can be booked back to back, which beats chasing seven men around the city.

## So when is it worth asking?

If getting to Vaughan is genuinely impossible rather than merely inconvenient, say so when you [book](/book). Sam will tell you straight whether he can work something out. What he won't do is promise a visit and then disappoint you, which is the more common story in this trade.

## The honest trade-off

An hour at the studio is the shortest version of this. Everything after it (the reorders, the second suit, your son's first jacket) is a phone call, because your pattern stays on file and nobody has to measure you again. The single visit is the cost of never having to do the visit again.

The studio is at 318 Charlton Avenue in Vaughan, on the Thornhill line, with parking at the door and a short run off Highway 7. Clients drive up from Toronto, North York, Richmond Hill and Markham without much drama.
